## 2. Claude's reflection

**What the completed work says**

- Riding is the anchor. Seven rides in a month, five of them an hour or more, without a plan telling you to. That is a base, and it is the one discipline where the Ironman distance is mostly time in the saddle. Keep protecting it.
- The planner is working as a training log. Sep 30, Oct 8 and Oct 11 are logged set by set, with RIR, and you left yourself notes mid-session. That is a better record than most coached athletes keep. Every future reflection gets sharper because of it.
- Strength A improved between its two runs: goblet squat up 5 lb, push-ups up 44%, heel drops at 90 lb with more reps. Two weeks apart, cleanly progressive. The tendon work is being done, which is the single most important injury-prevention item in the whole plan.
- The trainer got its first two sessions, including a full hour. That is the goal you named, and it is already started.

**Patterns worth noticing**

- The two continuous 3-mile runs on Sep 24 and 29 at 9:22 to 9:57 per mile, heart rate in the mid-140s to low-150s, are a lot more than the plan's 6 minutes of jogging. They show you have the engine. Whether the Achilles and knees agree is the question the pain log answers, not pace. The Sunday walk/run after them was the right call.
- Bench press tells the shoulder story in numbers: 145 lb on Sep 12, 120 lb on Sep 17, then 50 lb for high reps on Sep 24 and 29, then push-ups only. You adjusted before anyone told you to. Worth keeping that instinct for every pressing and overhead movement until the shoulder is quiet.
- Power on outdoor rides fell from 182 W to 143 W over the month. Some of that is likely the first days of keto (the lowest number is Oct 12). Some may be riding easier on purpose. Either way it is not fitness loss, and this is the window where you should expect it.
- Plan sessions moved around the week rather than being skipped: Strength A on Thursday instead of Monday, long ride on the trainer instead of outdoors. Moving is fine. The structure that matters is two rides, two runs, two strength, never two runs on consecutive days.

**Where the body is**

- Shoulder: unknown severity, but you are managing it by load selection. Rows and pulldowns are still in the log at full weight, so pulling seems fine and pressing is the sensitive direction. Swimming's catch phase loads exactly that pattern, so the swim pause is correct.
- Achilles and knees: no pain scores logged yet. The heel drops are at a sensible load and progressing. The step-downs went to 25 per side on Oct 11, which is well above the plan's 8 to 10; keep an eye on the knee the morning after high-rep step-downs.
- Keto adaptation: started early October, so the window runs to roughly mid-November. Expect flatter power, higher heart rate at the same effort, and harder second halves of rides for the next 3 to 5 weeks. Electrolytes matter more than usual during this phase. Keep every session in Zone 1 to 2 until it passes.

**What to carry forward**

- Keep two rides a week. Make the trainer ride ERG mode, Zone 2, 45 to 60 minutes, and skip the formal FTP test until keto adaptation is through. Use a provisional FTP of about 190 W (from your hour-long outdoor rides at 160 to 180 W average) which puts Zone 2 at roughly 105 to 140 W. Test properly in late November.
- Run only as walk/run, twice a week, never consecutive days, and write the pain score in the planner note. The 3-mile continuous runs can come back once a month of walk/run has been pain-free.
- Strength A and B stay. Your note "replace glute bridge with weighted glute thrusts on the machine" is adopted for Month 2. Keep push-ups only while they are pain-free; swap for a neutral-grip dumbbell floor press if the shoulder objects.
- No swimming until two weeks of pain-free pressing and daily life. The first swim back is 20 minutes of kick and drills, no pulling, and the next reflection decides what follows.
- Month 2 (November) will be rewritten as a keto-adaptation block: swim removed, trainer-centred, Zone 2 only, strength kept, run progression paused at the Week 1 to 2 level until pain scores are in.

## 4. Agreed adjustments for the next period

_Agreed 15 October 2026 from your comments. These are what Month 2 (2 November to 6 December, Weeks 5 to 9) is built on._

- **Keto-adaptation block with a fast-legs element.** Your comment: intervals and fast feet / fast legs matter for staying fast. Agreed, with one distinction: fast feet is cadence, not speed. Month 2 keeps every ride and run aerobic (Zone 1 to 2) but adds neuromuscular work that costs almost nothing metabolically: 30-second high-cadence spin-ups on the trainer, and 180 steps-per-minute cadence blocks on the treadmill at easy speed. No FTP test until the adaptation window has passed (target: Week 9 or Month 3).
- **Rides:** weekday trainer ride 45 to 60 min in ERG mode with spin-ups to kill the boredom, plus a weekend ride of 60 to 90 min, outdoors when the weather allows.

Month 2 caps the jog at 6.5 to 7.0 mph (about 8:35 to 9:15 per mile) and gets the "fast" feeling from cadence instead. If four weeks of that stay pain-free, the cap rises in Month 3.
